## 1. Evaluation Methodology

In educational data mining and academic risk prediction, standard evaluation practices (such as raw accuracy) can be dangerous and misleading:
- A cohort where $90\%$ of students pass would yield $90\%$ accuracy with a trivial dummy model that predicts zero risk for every student.
- Such a model completely fails the pedagogical mission: identifying the $10\%$ of students who urgently need academic assistance.

Therefore, MASTERY prioritizes:
1. **Sensitivity / Recall on the At-Risk Class:** Minimizing False Negatives ($\text{FN}$) so that struggling students are not missed.
2. **Probability Calibration (Brier Score):** Ensuring that a calculated risk score of $0.70$ means that approximately $70\%$ of students with this score historically experienced academic deficit.
3. **ROC-AUC (Discrimination):** Assessing how well the model separates at-risk students from on-track peers across all possible decision thresholds.

## 2. Evaluation Metrics Suite

All quantitative evaluation runs are persisted to `risk_model_evaluations`:

### 2.1 Confusion Matrix Structure
$$\begin{pmatrix} 
\text{TN (Passed, Predicted On-Track)} & \text{FP (Passed, Predicted At-Risk)} \\ 
\text{FN (Deficit, Predicted On-Track)} & \text{TP (Deficit, Predicted At-Risk)} 
\end{pmatrix}$$

### 2.2 Core Performance Indicators
- **Recall (Sensitivity):**
  $$\text{Recall} = \frac{\text{TP}}{\text{TP} + \text{FN}}$$
  *Pedagogical Goal:* $\ge 0.75$. We want to catch at least 3 out of every 4 students who would otherwise fail.
- **Precision (Positive Predictive Value):**
  $$\text{Precision} = \frac{\text{TP}}{\text{TP} + \text{FP}}$$
  *Goal:* $\ge 0.65$. Avoid alert fatigue for course faculty.
- **F1 Score:**
  $$\text{F1} = 2 \cdot \frac{\text{Precision} \cdot \text{Recall}}{\text{Precision} + \text{Recall}}$$
- **Brier Score (Calibration Loss):**
  $$\text{BS} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N (P_i - y_i)^2$$
  Where $P_i \in [0.0, 1.0]$ is predicted probability and $y_i \in \{0, 1\}$ is empirical milestone outcome. A lower score indicates better probability calibration ($\text{BS} < 0.20$).

## 3. Risk Classification Threshold Mapping

Probabilities generated by active models are mapped to intuitive, actionable risk categories:

| Probability Range ($P_{\text{risk}}$) | Risk Tier | Evidence Required | Recommended Institutional Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $[0.75, 1.00]$ | **CRITICAL** | Strong ($\ge 3$ assessments) | Immediate 1-on-1 faculty advising; remedial tutorial assignment |
| $[0.55, 0.75)$ | **HIGH** | Moderate / Strong | Targeted peer tutoring; prioritized lab assistance |
| $[0.30, 0.55)$ | **MODERATE** | Moderate | Self-paced review recommendations on flagged topic gaps |
| $[0.00, 0.30)$ | **LOW** | Moderate | Standard coursework continuation; elective enrichment |
| N/A | **INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE** | Insufficient ($< 2$ assessments) | Schedule diagnostic evaluation before forming predictive assessment |

## 4. Cross-Validation and Sample Splitting Protocol

1. **Stratified K-Fold Cross-Validation ($k = 5$):** When historical cohort size $\ge 30$, stratified cross-validation preserves minority class ratio across all training and test folds.
2. **Temporal Split Validation:** When multiple historical semesters are available (e.g. Fall 2024, Fall 2025), models are trained on older terms and evaluated on the most recent completed term to simulate true production forecasting conditions.
3. **Ablation Studies:** Validating feature groups to ensure that attendance telemetry does not dominate conceptual mastery signals.
